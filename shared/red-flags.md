# Shared Red Flags and Escalation

A single, auditable checklist of escalation triggers, consolidated from the
specialist agents. Every agent screens against this before giving training,
nutrition, sleep or movement advice. This complements `safety-boundaries.md`;
where they overlap, both apply.

> Educational decision support, not diagnosis. When in doubt, escalate. Adapt
> the exact service (emergency number, urgent care, GP) to the user's location
> recorded in `user-profile.md`.

## Emergency — advise urgent/emergency care now

- Chest pain, pressure or tightness, especially with exertion.
- Severe or sudden breathlessness; breathing difficulty.
- Fainting, blackout or collapse.
- Stroke-like signs: facial droop, arm weakness, speech difficulty, sudden
  severe headache, or progressive neurological symptoms.
- Major trauma or suspected fracture.
- Severe allergic reaction.
- Suicidal intent or immediate danger to self or others.

## Urgent — advise timely professional assessment

- Persistent, worsening, unexplained or recurrent symptoms.
- Concerning or irregular palpitations; unexplained performance collapse.
- Suspected sleep apnoea (loud snoring, witnessed pauses, unrefreshing sleep,
  severe daytime sleepiness).
- Persistent insomnia or prolonged unexplained fatigue.
- Eating-disorder patterns or risk.
- Meaningful medication interactions, including with supplements.
- New neurological symptoms (numbness, tingling, weakness, radiating pain).
- Any condition that materially changes exercise or nutrition safety.

## During exercise — stop and reassess

- Sharp, escalating or joint-specific pain (distinct from ordinary effort or
  post-exercise soreness).
- Neurological symptoms provoked by movement.
- Symptoms from the emergency list — stop and seek care.

## Absolute don'ts (all agents)

- Never advise stopping prescribed medication.
- Never encourage crash diets, purging or severe dehydration.
- Never advise training through sharp or escalating pain.
- Never advise ignoring neurological symptoms.
- Never diagnose or imply a specific diagnosis.

## Acceptable-response framing (movement/training)

When adapting training, define:

- The acceptable symptom response *during* exercise.
- The acceptable response over the next **24–48 hours**.
- Clear **stop** and **escalation** criteria.
